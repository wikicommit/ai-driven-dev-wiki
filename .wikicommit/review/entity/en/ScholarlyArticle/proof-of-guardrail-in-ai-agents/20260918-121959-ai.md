Key point 6 says the latency overhead ranged "from 24.8% to 38.0% across the four tasks tested". Those four figures are the four rows of Table 2's "Task" column, but they are really two datasets (ToxicChat, FacTool-KBQA) each split into a guardrail row and a response-generation row - not four distinct tasks. A reader could take it as four separate agent tasks. The body prose's "25% to 38%" matches the paper's own Limitations wording verbatim, so the two figures in the page are not in conflict.
The Notes section relays the paper's characterization of "attestable audits" as the most relevant prior work and as "ensuring that a model a user communicates with has provably passed security audits". That characterization comes from a single passing related-work mention (Schnabl et al., 2025) in the source; the cited paper itself is not among the evidence. The page is careful to keep it in attributed form ("which they describe as", "they claim"), and it names no title or date, so it was not treated as a MISSING_SOURCE finding - but a later reader should know the wiki is relaying this paper's framing of another work it does not hold, not an independently checked fact.
This page uses "guardrail" in a narrow runtime sense (a content-safety or fact-checking filter that mediates an agent's inputs, outputs and tool calls), whereas the existing DefinedTerm/guardrails page defines the term much more broadly as an organization-level framework of policies, access controls, approval thresholds and audit logging. The two senses do not contradict each other on any shared fact, and this page does not link to that glossary entry - but the term carries noticeably different scope in the two places.
