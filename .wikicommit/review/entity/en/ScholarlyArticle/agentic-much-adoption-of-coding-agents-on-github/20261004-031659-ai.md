Key Points bullet 1 says 12.08% of the projects showed file-level traces 'and a further 11.51% only at the commit level'. The paper words it the same way in its introduction, but the 11.51% is a share of the 112,551 projects with no file-level traces, not of all 128,018 (Table 1, section 5.2). That is why 12.08% + 11.51% does not add up to the 22.20% conservative estimate. A reader may take both percentages as having the same denominator.
Notes says AGENTS.md is credited to Codex, 'which originally introduced it'. The source supports this ('the AGENTS.md file was originally introduced by Codex'). The linked [[DefinedTerm/agents-md]] page traces the standard to Sourcegraph/Amp's singular AGENT.md RFC and says Codex was already using the plural filename. These are not strictly the same fact (who introduced the filename versus where the standardization effort started), so this is not a contradiction. Still, a reader moving between the two pages may see a tension.
The last Key Points bullet compares against 'a prior study of human-authored commits' (Zeng et al. in the source). The page reports the paper's own conclusion ('about twice as common') and gives none of that study's figures, so it stays within the source. It already flags that the comparison depends on the other study.
