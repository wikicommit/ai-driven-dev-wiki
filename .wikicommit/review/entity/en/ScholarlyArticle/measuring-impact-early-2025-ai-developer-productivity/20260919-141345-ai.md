The opening paragraph says each issue was "assigned to a condition by a simulated coin flip" without the paper's footnote recording that 25 issues early in the study were randomized differently (the paper shows results are insensitive to their inclusion). The page reads as if the procedure was uniform across all 246 issues.
The Key Points bullet gives "developers accepting under 44% of AI generations" flatly; the paper notes this statistic excludes 3 developers who used their own pre-existing Cursor Pro subscriptions. The "9% of time spent reviewing or cleaning it" figure is computed on a subset of 44 issues with valid labeled screen recordings, which the page does not qualify — a reader may attach it to the 74-recording subset named in the preceding bullet.
"Developers slowed down more on issues they knew better" compresses the paper's two separate self-reported measures (high prior task exposure and low external resource needs), and drops the paper's own hedge that this is only "moderate evidence".
