Body paragraph 1 says the authors observe that 'none had isolated' the effect of adding an AGENTS.md file. The source hedges this twice: 'we are not aware of studies that isolate the impact of adding an AGENTS.md file' (source lines 73-75 and 122-124). The page states it slightly more strongly than the paper does. 'Were not aware of any study that had isolated' would match the source exactly.
Body paragraph 2 says 'AGENTS.md was first used with Codex before becoming an open format'. The paper does say this (source lines 123-125, 'AGENTS.md was first used with Codex before becoming an open format'), and the page correctly gives it as one of the authors' reasons for choosing Codex. The one-hop page DefinedTerm/agents-md.md tells a different origin story: the standard began as Sourcegraph/Amp's singular AGENT.md RFC, and that page also notes that Codex was already using the plural filename. The two accounts do not contradict each other on the same fact, since AGENT.md and AGENTS.md are different filenames. A reader could still see a tension, so keeping this sentence attributed to the authors, as it is now, matters.
Key Points bullet 5 says the medians 'were essentially unchanged or slightly higher with AGENTS.md, as was the median of total tokens'. For input, cached input and total tokens, the medians are all slightly higher with AGENTS.md (-3.41%, -0.99%, -1.29% in Table 1). The prose 'essentially unchanged or slightly higher' comes from source lines 296-297 and applies only to input and cached input. The total-tokens part comes from the table alone. It is supported, but it is lumped into the paper's own phrasing.
