The '5.3 times more tokens than an eviction-free cache' figure matches Section 7.3 and Table 12 of the source. The paper's own introduction gives a different number: 'cache misses cause 3.8x more tokens to be prefilled than prefills due to truly unique input tokens'. The page uses the detailed section's figure, which is reasonable, but the source contradicts itself on this point.
The claim that calls longer than one minute are about 4% of calls and 85% of tool-call time comes from the introduction's combined figure. Section 6.2 gives different per-provider figures: Claude 4.9% of calls and 92% of time, Codex 3.1% and 61%. A reader comparing the page with the body of the paper could be confused.
The Notes section describes SWE-chat and similar datasets as analysing 'behavioural or code-change outcomes' rather than the interaction timeline. This description comes only from the paper's related-work section. There the phrase 'code-change outcomes ... but not the interaction timeline or serving system' refers to AgentPack and 'The Rise of AI Teammates', while SWE-chat is described as analysing behavioural outcomes (vibe-coding, code survival, security). The merged wording is acceptable because it is framed as the paper's own positioning. The SWE-chat document itself is not among the evidence.
The description calls the paper 'University of Washington-led'. The source gives only author affiliations: most authors are at UW, and a footnote says Chenxi Ma's work was done while interning at UW. The source never says who led the work, so 'led' is an inference from the affiliations.
