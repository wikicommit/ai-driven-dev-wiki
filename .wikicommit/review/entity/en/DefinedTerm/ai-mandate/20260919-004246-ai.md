The closing paragraph of "When It Applies" opens "Two failure modes are named." The first, the productivity pressure paradox, is indeed named by the paper (via Miller et al.) as a mandate failure mode. The second, measurement gaming, is not presented by the paper as a named failure mode of mandates but as a bound on its own external validity. The page's own next clause locates it correctly, so nothing is misstated, but the shared framing puts a mechanism and a study limitation under one heading.
"The concept is descriptive rather than prescriptive" sits two paragraphs after the page has reported the paper's explicit Implications for Practice recommendations. The sentence is presumably about the term being a descriptive label rather than a policy recommendation, but as written next to that guidance it is easy to read as a claim about the paper, which does prescribe.
"The authors describe a mandate documented that precisely, alongside multi-year telemetry from an identifiable firm, as scarce" shifts the source's predicate. The source says joining the two research strands "is scarce because it requires a documented mandate and multi-year telemetry from an identifiable firm" — scarcity is asserted of the research, with the rarity of the combination as the stated reason. The page asserts scarcity of the combination itself. Entailed, but a step the source takes only implicitly.
Both the frontmatter description and the lead paragraph render the upper pole of the range as "enforced firm-wide adoption"; the source's range statement is "from stated expectation to enforced adoption", with "firm-wide" belonging to the adjacent Coinbase clause. The added word narrows the pole slightly beyond the source's own formulation of the range.
