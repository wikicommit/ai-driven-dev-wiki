The page's Usage section opens with a flat "The environment is not designed up front but accumulates through governance-conversion". The source does say "The governed engineering environment emerges iteratively, shaped by joint human-agent capability" and that "many necessary controls cannot be fully specified before work begins", so the claim is supported — but the same paper also states (§VII-A-1, §VII-B-2) that the Subject did use ex-ante governance such as WCAG 2.1 AA conformance checkers and commodity lints, found necessary but not sufficient, and that both ex-ante and ex-post governance are needed; Fig. 1(c) describes the governance-centric model as defining a governed engineering environment *before* agent work begins. The page's unqualified "not designed up front" is therefore stronger than the paper's position and is easy to misread as ruling out ex-ante governance entirely.
The page's definition sentence and frontmatter description characterise the governed engineering environment with the word list the paper attaches to a differently-named construct: Fig. 4(b) defines "Engineering governance substrate" as "The harnessing, types, lints, schemas, deployment processes, etc. necessary for agent productivity." The paper uses the two terms close to interchangeably (§VI-B-1, Fig. 3, §X), so this is not a misstatement, but the page silently merges two of the paper's labels and replaces the paper's open-ended "etc." with the specific "and analyses" (which §V-C's static/dynamic analysis figures do support).
