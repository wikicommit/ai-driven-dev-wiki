The sentence "agents repeatedly violated constraints they had never been told about" sharpens the source. §V-B2 says the relevant context already existed in the repository but was distributed across component models, lint declarations, repair documentation and project conventions, and that the engineering problem was to make that context selectable and inject it before work began — the constraints were undelivered at dispatch time rather than nonexistent. A reader could take the page's phrasing to mean the rules had not been written down anywhere.
"Its characteristic failure is to read a failure as a local defect and patch it, rather than as evidence of a missing abstraction or an underspecified interface" is the page's own synthesis rather than a single source sentence: it combines the Authority moderator ("lest structural failures get local patches"), §V-B's "interpreted failures as structural problems rather than isolated defects", and §VII-A2's list ("ambiguities, missing abstractions, underspecified interfaces, and coordination gaps"). The content is carried by the source, but the paper never labels this a characteristic failure mode of the process.
The frontmatter `description` presents governance conversion only as "an ex-post process of discovering controls from failures that only become visible once agents are working at speed". That is faithful to the abstract, but the paper's own position (§VII-B1) is that both ex-ante and ex-post governance are needed; read on its own, the description could suggest ex-post governance supplants ex-ante. The body text does state the "both are needed" point correctly.
