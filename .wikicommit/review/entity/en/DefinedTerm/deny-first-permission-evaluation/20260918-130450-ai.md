Round 1 (FAIL) found a CONTRADICTION: the draft said the posture is "motivated less by distrust of the model than by evidence about people". The paper never ranks the two motivations and elsewhere presents deny-first as expressing a threat model that assumes an untrusted model. The comparison was removed and both motivations are now stated without ranking.
The page calls the independence-assumption problem "Its central weakness". The paper introduces it with "More fundamentally" and elevates the shared-failure-mode question to "the relevant evaluation criterion", which supports the emphasis, but it never ranks weaknesses and presents the pre-trust initialization gap as a separate finding without placing it below this one. The superlative is the page's framing, not the paper's.
The opening paragraph states unconditionally that an action matching no explicit rule "is escalated to a human rather than permitted by default", while the Usage section correctly notes that the active permission mode determines baseline handling. In the source that baseline varies by mode (dontAsk auto-denies; bypassPermissions skips most prompts). A reader stopping at the lead sentence could take escalation to be absolute rather than the default-mode behaviour.
