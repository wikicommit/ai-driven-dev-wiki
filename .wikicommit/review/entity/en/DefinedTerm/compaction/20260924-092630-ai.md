The new TraceLab paragraph quotes the paper's Section 4.2 body ('Compaction is not rare but not dominant either'). The same paper's own Takeaways bullet in Section 4.5 is headed 'Compaction is rare.', so the source contradicts itself on this point. The page follows the body text, which carries the 9.7% / 3.7 / 18.4% vs 4.5% figures, and that is the better-supported wording. A later reader may still meet the 'rare' framing if they quote the takeaway.
'about 4,300 real sessions of Claude Code and OpenAI Codex' is accurate. However, the paper says the trace comes from the authors' 'own day-to-day use' (43 developers, mostly research workflows), and its Limitations section warns the results 'may not fully generalize to other organizations'. Read without that context, 'real sessions' could suggest a broader population than the one the paper measured.
