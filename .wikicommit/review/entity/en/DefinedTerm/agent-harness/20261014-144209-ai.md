The opening sentence and the `description` property give one source's definition without saying whose it is. The phrase 'turns a stateless language model into a functional agent by grounding its outputs in external execution, persistent state and verifiable feedback' is the code-as-agent-harness survey's own wording (pdf-2605.18747.md line 286). 'Policy-governed system' is also the survey's phrase (line 760). Other sources say much the same thing: Trivedy says a harness gives the model 'state, tool execution, feedback loops'; OpenHarness calls it 'complete infrastructure ... to make it a functional agent'. So stating the core claim without attribution is acceptable. However, the page later stresses that definitions differ, and 'policy-governed' in particular is the survey's own framing.
In the Usage section, 'The survey' first appears right after a paragraph about the Gorinova et al. position paper. It refers back to [[ScholarlyArticle/code-as-agent-harness]], which was introduced two paragraphs earlier. A reader could take it to mean the Gorinova paper, which is a position paper and not a survey.
'The authors call this executable accountability' attaches the term to the argument that human-in-the-loop decisions should become durable harness state and that approvals should be auditable. In the source (lines 2970-2973), 'executable accountability' is defined as 'a safety layer that filters, vetoes, escalates, and records agent actions before they reach the real world'. The two ideas are close, but the page's mapping is slightly looser than the source's definition.
