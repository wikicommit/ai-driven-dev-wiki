The Usage section links the S/N concept to [[DefinedTerm/signal-to-noise-ratio]], but that existing page is titled 'Signal-to-Noise Ratio (Code Review)'. It defines the term only as the share of actionable review comments on a pull request. The source means something different: the share of task-relevant information (signal) against unrelated information and redundant logs (noise) in an LLM's context (source line 330). A reader who follows the link will land on the other meaning. The two pages state no conflicting fact, so this is not a check-8 finding, but the link target is easy to misread.
The last sentence of Usage, 'The remedy the post proposes is to split such work into commands with a single responsibility each and to inject only the minimum information a task needs', joins two separate practices from the source. In the source, the single-responsibility split is the fix given for the web-research example (lines 402-409). Minimising context is a separate best practice under its own heading (5-2, lines 415-419). It is not presented as part of that example's remedy. Both points appear in the source, but together in one sentence they read as a single fix proposed for that example.
