Every figure the page quotes was checked against the measurement study and matches: 1.2B URLs, six objective categories, 99% task override with jailbreak reinforcement, ~70% in non-rendered channels, 54 templates covering 95% of instances, 65% of pages carrying an injection 12 months earlier, 5,200 trials across 13 models and four representations, 8% peak for small models on plain text, 0.2%-1.1% where structural cues are preserved. Attribution stays correctly bound between the two sources throughout.
The Usage section opens with "Beyond the adversarial framing the term was coined under". Neither source states that anyone coined the term: the first says only "We reveal new attack vectors, using Indirect Prompt Injection", and the second introduces it as a class of attacks with a multi-reference citation rather than crediting a coinage. The substantive origin claim ("introduced by Greshake et al.") is a fair reading and matches the linked article page, but "coined" asserts a naming event no evidence records; "introduced" would be the safer word.
"among those embedded in rendered HTML the great majority are concealed by ... colour and contrast manipulation, occlusion and viewport-based hiding" compresses a narrower result. The 58.6% concealed / ~5.1% visible split comes from a live re-rendering, in January 2026, of the ~3.2K-page subset of HTML-injection pages still reachable, and in ~34% of those cases the prompt was no longer present at all. The paper's own prose carries the claim, so this is the page reading as a stronger, unconditional statement than the measurement it rests on.
