Lead paragraph says pull-request volume grew 3.1x "over the study window". The source measures that 3.1x against the early-2025 (Jan-Apr 2025) baseline shown in Figure 4, whereas its stated study window is January 2024-April 2026. The multiplier is right; the period label is looser than the source's.
The opening definition sentence is near-verbatim from the source's own sentence ("AI accelerates authoring while human review capacity stays fixed, so surplus work accumulates downstream") and is stated as unqualified general fact; attribution to the paper only arrives in the following sentence. It reads as an established term rather than the one study's formulation until that second sentence lands.
The per-PR latency paragraph (~20% review lead, ~22% total cycle) omits two caveats the source attaches to exactly those numbers: the per-PR estimates are correlational rather than causal (the created-by-ai label is applied outside the authors' control), and late-window AI-driven review and auto-approval bias the measured premium toward zero, making it conservative. The organization-level and quality paragraphs do carry the source's hedges, so caveat coverage is uneven across sections.
"AI-labelled pull requests" is used for what the source calls AI-authored PRs (those carrying the company's internal created-by-ai label). The page never explains the label, so a reader may take it as a claim about authorship rather than about an internal tagging convention.
