## Summary

This systematic literature review synthesises 92 primary studies on agentic AI across the software development life cycle, screened from more than 1,600 candidate publications using a purpose-built multi-agent LLM pipeline. It identifies output verifiability as the primary enabler of agentic adoption: later lifecycle phases whose outputs are objectively evaluable through executable feedback show the highest maturity and industrial presence, while earlier phases remain almost exclusively academic proofs-of-concept. It reports Planner-Executor-Reviewer role specialisation as the dominant architectural pattern and finds that industrial mitigation strategies converge on confining agent actions to verifiable, bounded spaces.
