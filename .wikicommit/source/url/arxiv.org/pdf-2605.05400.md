## Summary

This paper, published for VibeX 2026, proposes mise en place (MEP), a preparation-first methodology for agentic coding borrowed from the culinary practice of setting everything in place before cooking, as a response to the alignment problem the author attributes to vibe coding. MEP has three phases completed before implementation begins — contextual grounding (externalizing domain expertise and tacit knowledge into documents), collaborative specification (human-agent dialogue producing design artifacts) and task decomposition (dependency-aware task records) — and the paper reports applying it at a five-hour hackathon, where about two hours of preparation enabled four parallel agents to build a full-stack educational platform. It also introduces context fluency, the skill of creating rich, structured context that agents can act on, acknowledges that its evidence is a single uncontrolled case study, and sets out a research agenda for validating preparation-phase methods.
