## Summary
A conceptual paper from Universidad Politécnica de Madrid, based on a multivocal literature review that draws largely on gray literature, which argues that the move from AI-assisted practices such as vibe coding to agentic software engineering is a socio-technical reconfiguration of the team, and proposes Spec-Driven Development as the discipline that makes it workable at team scale. It presents a socio-technical model in which specifications are the contract substrate between humans and agents, distinguishes a transient technical harness around the agent from a durable methodological harness of eight team-owned mechanisms, and sets out five human–agent interaction patterns (briefing, consultation, review, norm encoding and orchestration). The authors present the framework as falsifiable hypotheses rather than a validated theory and outline a research agenda for testing it.
