## Summary

A September 2026 UC Berkeley preprint arguing that agentic software engineering's main failure modes are explained by two gaps outside the implementation-verification loop: a requirement gap between stakeholder intent and recorded requirements, and a model gap between the real deployment world and the environment model assumed by the evaluator. Reward hacking exploits these gaps and hallucination widens them; since neither can generally be certified closed in an open, changing world, the authors propose an outer assurance-revision loop driven by stakeholder judgment and deployment evidence, and frame assured agentic development as a resource-allocation problem over human judgment, agent capability and compute.
