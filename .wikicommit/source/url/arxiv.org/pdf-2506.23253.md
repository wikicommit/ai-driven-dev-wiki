## Summary

Sarkar and Drosos's PPIG 2025 paper presents what it calls the first empirical study of vibe coding, a framework analysis of about 8.5 hours of think-aloud vibe coding videos from YouTube and Twitch. It finds that vibe coding follows iterative goal-satisfaction cycles, that prompts mix vague directives with detailed specifications, and that programming expertise is redistributed toward context management, rapid code evaluation and deciding when to switch to manual work rather than eliminated. The authors frame vibe coding as an early manifestation of material disengagement and name related phenomena such as context momentum.
