## Summary

A survey of agentic tool use in large language models that organises the literature into three methodological paradigms: prompting as plug-and-play (a frozen model guided by in-context scaffolds), supervised tool learning (tool-use behaviour internalised into weights through labelled or synthetic supervision) and reward-driven tool policy learning (multi-turn tool interaction optimised by reinforcement learning). It treats evaluation as a cross-cutting dimension, grouping benchmarks into tool usage correctness, task completion and tool-driven interaction, and argues that the three paradigms are complementary rather than successive. It closes with five future directions, including protocol standardisation, multimodal and embodied foundation models, continuous self-evolution, safety, and human-agent symbiosis.
