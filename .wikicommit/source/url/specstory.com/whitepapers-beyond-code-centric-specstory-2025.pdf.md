## Summary

A SpecStory whitepaper of 15 June 2025 by co-founder Greg Ceccarelli, arguing that AI coding agents have moved software's bottleneck from development speed to specification clarity — the "abstraction shift" — and that Waterfall, Agile and GitFlow each fail in a multi-voice agent environment because all three assume a human will interpret an ambiguous instruction rather than act on it literally. Its proposed answer is trunk-based development with versioned specifications from every role, operationalised by SpecStory's own five-step Specflow workflow, and a three-layer stack of declared intent, code and tests. Its most useful contribution to this wiki is its refusal to declare the problem solved: it catalogues the residual micro-decisions the approach demands and concludes that spec-driven development with agents "remains powerful in theory but insufficient in practice" until better intent-centric tooling exists — a qualification now recorded on the spec-driven development page.

Four entities discussed as foils rather than as independent subjects — Waterfall, Agile, GitFlow and trunk-based development — did not become pages. Each is a pre-existing general software methodology whose treatment here is one vendor's advocacy about how it fares with agents, so their material is recorded on the whitepaper and Specflow pages instead of letting a single provider's account become the wiki's primary definition of a general term.
