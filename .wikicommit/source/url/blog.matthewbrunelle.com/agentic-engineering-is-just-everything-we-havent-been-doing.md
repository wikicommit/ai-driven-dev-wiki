## Summary

An opinion post arguing that the practices people adopt to make agentic engineering work are the ordinary software engineering practices teams routinely cut under time pressure — issue tracking, docstrings, current documentation, meaningful tests, agreed conventions, design review, recorded decisions, open channels. Its thesis is that agentic engineering is just normal engineering with robots, and its corollary that anything making engineering harder makes agentic engineering much harder, because an agent lacks the situational judgement a person brings to a broken environment. It also records one genuine change: encoding principles in documentation now has an effect it previously would not, because every engineer's agent reads it.
