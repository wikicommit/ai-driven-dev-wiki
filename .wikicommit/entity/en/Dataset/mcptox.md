MCPTox is a benchmark, introduced in [[ScholarlyArticle/mcptox-a-benchmark-for-tool-poisoning-attack-on-real-world-mcp-servers]], for evaluating how robust LLM agents are against [[DefinedTerm/tool-poisoning]] — malicious instructions embedded in a tool's metadata — in realistic [[DefinedTerm/model-context-protocol]] settings. Its authors describe it as the first benchmark to evaluate this threat systematically.

## Contents

The benchmark is constructed on 45 live, real-world MCP servers and 353 authentic tools. It comprises 1312 malicious test cases covering 10 categories of potential risk.

## Provenance

The test cases were generated by few-shot learning from three distinct attack templates designed by the authors. The paper states that the dataset is available at an anonymized repository.

## Use

[[ScholarlyArticle/mcptox-a-benchmark-for-tool-poisoning-attack-on-real-world-mcp-servers]] uses MCPTox to evaluate 20 prominent LLM agent settings and reports widespread vulnerability to tool poisoning, including an attack success rate of 72.8% for o1-mini and refusal rates below 3% for every model evaluated.
