A vision paper by Rashina Hoda of Monash University, accepted to the AGENT workshop at the International Conference on Software Engineering (ICSE) 2026. Its argument is that early visions of [[DefinedTerm/agentic-software-engineering]] are concentrated almost entirely on code-related activities — code generation, review, debugging, repair, configuration — while requirements engineering, design, operations, and practical concerns such as adoption, teamwork, management, and workflow integration go unaddressed.

The paper's stated position is that for agentic SE to become the next process-level paradigm shift rather than merely a coding accelerator, its scope must expand to the full range of socio-technical activities, artefacts, roles, and concerns across the SE lifecycle — traditional ones renewed for agents and multi-agent systems, plus new ones needed to make human-agent ecosystems work. It grounds this in a review of SE's own history, noting that every previous SE process model, from Waterfall through Agile, featured a 'whole of process' approach.

## Key Contributions

- **A 'whole of process' vision** for agentic SE, presented explicitly as a preliminary 'first look' rather than a definitive process model. It spans five high-level areas approached iteratively by human and agent actors at varying levels of AI agency under human control: Agentic SE Ethical Alignment (placed first and central), Agentic Requirements Engineering, Agentic Design, Agentic Development, and Agentic Operations.
- **The CRAFT values and principles** — a preliminary set of five values, each with two principles, offered to spark community conversation. The values are Comprehensive (whole of process approach; human, agentic and socio-technical aspects), Responsible (ethics-by-design; sustainability), Adaptive (industrial relevance; socio-technical relevance), Foundational (foundational knowledge and solutions beyond coding), and Translational (awareness and education; actionable guidelines).
- **Guidance on agentic SE vocabulary**, motivated by the observation that terminology drifts and discrepancies are already apparent in a nascent field — some syntactic, such as agentic AI software engineer versus AI software engineer versus agentic software engineer, and some reflecting deeper semantic and philosophical issues. Five considerations are proposed for designing terminology: relevance, coverage, acceptance, consistency, and philosophical alignment.

## Notes

The paper is a position and vision piece, not an empirical study: the author states the proposed ideas are not meant to be exhaustive or final but to encourage community feedback, and concludes that we may not know what agentic SE truly looks like until it is studied empirically 'in the wild'. It calls for longitudinal studies of agentic repositories and forums, and in-depth empirical work based on interviews, surveys, and experiments with agentic SE teams.
