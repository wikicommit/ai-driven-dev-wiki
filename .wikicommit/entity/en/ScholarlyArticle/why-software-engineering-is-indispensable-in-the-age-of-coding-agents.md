The paper asks whether AI can make software engineering — the discipline — obsolete, and whether it
can make software engineers — the professionals — redundant. Its answer is no on both counts: it
argues that the rise of capable AI coding agents makes software engineering and software engineers
essential, as the missing foundation without which AI-assisted development produces software that
is misleadingly plausible, unverifiable and ultimately untrustworthy.

The argument runs from the nature of large language models to the role of the engineer. The paper
identifies three structural properties of these models — probabilistic generation, agnosticism and
semantic statelessness — that, in its account, create a "structural vacuum" no amount of training
can eliminate. Filling that vacuum, it proposes, requires four knowledge levers, all of which must be
reified as persistent artifacts, and each of which requires the software engineer in three roles.

## Key Points

- The paper claims that capable AI coding agents make software engineering and software engineers
  essential, not obsolete.
- Without that foundation, according to the paper, AI-assisted development produces misleadingly
  plausible, unverifiable and ultimately untrustworthy software.
- It names three structural properties of large language models — probabilistic generation,
  agnosticism and semantic statelessness — as creating a structural vacuum that no amount of
  training can eliminate.
- It proposes four knowledge levers to fill that vacuum: methodological knowledge, domain knowledge,
  design choices and process choices.
- It holds that all four levers must be reified as persistent artifacts, and that each requires the
  software engineer acting as methodologist, mediator and custodian.
