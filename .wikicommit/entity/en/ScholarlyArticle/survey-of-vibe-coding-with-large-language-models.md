A survey paper on [[DefinedTerm/vibe-coding]] with large language models, submitted to arXiv on 14 October 2025 and last revised on 21 December 2025. The authors present it as the first comprehensive and systematic review of the topic, aiming to establish both theoretical foundations and practical frameworks for the approach.

The survey's starting premise is that the advancement of LLMs has catalyzed a paradigm shift from code generation assistance to autonomous coding agents, enabling a development methodology the authors term vibe coding, in which developers validate AI-generated implementations through outcome observation rather than line-by-line code comprehension. It notes that despite the approach's transformative potential its effectiveness remains under-explored, with empirical evidence revealing unexpected productivity losses and fundamental challenges in human-AI collaboration — the gap the survey sets out to address.

## Key Contributions

- A systematic analysis drawing on over 1000 research papers, surveying the vibe coding ecosystem across its critical infrastructure components: LLMs for coding, LLM-based coding agents, the development environment of coding agents, and feedback mechanisms.
- An introduction of vibe coding as a formal discipline, formalized through a Constrained Markov Decision Process that captures what the authors describe as the dynamic triadic relationship among human developers, software projects, and coding agents.
- A taxonomy synthesizing existing practice into five distinct development models: Unconstrained Automation, Iterative Conversational Collaboration, Planning-Driven, Test-Driven, and Context-Enhanced Models. The authors present this as the first comprehensive taxonomy in the domain.
- A concluding analysis arguing that successful vibe coding depends not merely on agent capabilities but on systematic context engineering, well-established development environments, and human-agent collaborative development models.
