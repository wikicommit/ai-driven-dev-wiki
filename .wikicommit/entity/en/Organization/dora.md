DORA — the DevOps Research and Assessment group — is a research programme that publishes annual survey-based research on software delivery performance, and whose 2025 report is devoted to AI-assisted software development. That is not its first work on the subject: the foreword to that report records that "in 2024, DORA released a landmark report measuring the effects of AI on software delivery performance, one of the first systematic studies of its kind", and the 2025 chapters repeatedly compare their findings against the previous year's. Its stated purpose, in its own words, is that it "has long strived not just to describe the state of software delivery, but to help organizations make data-backed decisions about how to navigate an ever-changing landscape of development tools, techniques, and technologies."

## History

The origin the report gives is the 2013 State of DevOps research. [[Person/gene-kim]] recounts in the foreword to [[Report/state-of-ai-assisted-software-development-2025]] that in 2013 he worked with Dr. Nicole Forsgren and Jez Humble on that research, and that "this work became the basis for the DevOps Research and Assessment group—DORA—which became part of Google Cloud in 2018." The source gives no founding date for the group itself; 2018 is the only date it attaches to DORA as an entity.

The finding he recalls from that period frames what the programme has been for since: that doing multiple deployments per day was not a crazy idea, that reliability seemed to require smaller and more frequent deployments, and that achieving this did not require being a startup or being in Silicon Valley — only great technical practices such as automated builds, testing and deployments and proactive production telemetry, an architecture enabling independence of action, and a culture of learning.

## Activities & Products

The annual report is the main output. [[Report/state-of-ai-assisted-software-development-2025]] draws on a global survey conducted between 13 June and 21 July 2025 with nearly 5,000 technology professionals and more than 100 hours of qualitative data, and introduces the [[DefinedTerm/dora-ai-capabilities-model]] — DORA's first model of the conditions under which AI adoption pays off — alongside seven team archetypes derived from cluster analysis. DORA describes the AI Capabilities Model as something it will "continue validating, revising, and refining" with further research, as it has with its existing DORA Core Model.

Its research team is named in that report: Derek DeBellis has led DORA's research programme since 2022, co-leading with Dr. Kevin M. Storer, who leads ethnographic research for the team, and Nathen Harvey leads the DORA team at Google Cloud.

DORA also runs a community programme, which the report describes as a platform for professionals to engage with the research and apply it in their own organizations, offering learning from guest speakers and other members and early access to new research.
