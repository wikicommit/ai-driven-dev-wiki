CyberAgent appears in this wiki as an adopter of AI-driven development practice rather than as a vendor of tooling. The account available here comes from inside the company — a post on its own developers' blog — so what it establishes is how one of its engineers describes the organization, not an outside assessment of it.

Three organization-level facts are stated. The company is described as having a culture of promoting AI adoption irrespective of job function. Its annual investment in introducing development agents is given as ¥400 million. And internal AI usage is made visible across the company, concretely as a ranking of tool usage: the post's author reports having placed second company-wide for [[SoftwareApplication/claude-code]] usage, while noting that the denominator was masked.

## Background

The post used here is written from the partnered retail-media division (協業リテールメディア div.) of the company's AI business unit (AI 事業本部), which builds and operates a reward-type advertising service for retailers' own apps — the stated aim being to lead customers to a purchase once they understand the product.

That service is described as spanning ad delivery servers, in-app web views styled to each retailer's brand, an SDK for integrating into an app, an admin console for submitting ads, and an analytics platform over delivery results and retail point-of-sale data. The author gives that spread as the reason the work demands backend, frontend, native and data-science skills alongside domain knowledge of retail, and names Sapporo Drug Store and Yamada Denki as retailers it has been deployed with.

The same post supplies the organizational condition its practice was a response to: the service launched with six engineers and three business staff, and at the time of writing ran with two engineers and one business staff member, without a corresponding reduction in the advertising campaigns and initiatives it had to deliver.
