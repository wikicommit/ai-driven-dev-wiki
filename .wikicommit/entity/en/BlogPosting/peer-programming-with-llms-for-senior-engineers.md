Peter Mbanugo's post, dated May 24, 2025, opens by calling programming with LLMs "both promising and
frustrating": the assistants can help with coding and debugging but often waste time too, yet for senior
engineers he sees real potential in what he calls "peer programming" with LLMs, striking through the
word "pair". The rest of the post is a curated collection of blog posts by other senior or staff+
engineers about using LLMs in their work, which he presents as free of "the usual hype or buzzwords".

Each entry is a short summary of someone else's post plus Mbanugo's own reaction to it. The post makes no
empirical claims of its own; its contribution is the selection and the author's first-hand remarks about
which techniques he has tried.

## Key Points

- Sean Goedecke's post on practical AI techniques is summarized as describing two main ways he uses AI:
  a "second opinion" technique and a "throwaway debugging scripts" technique. Mbanugo reports having used
  the "second opinion" technique himself in recent weeks and wanting to try the second one.
- Harper Reed's LLM codegen workflow is summarized as: brainstorm a spec, co-plan a plan, then execute
  using LLM codegen, and repeat. Mbanugo likes the brainstorm-and-plan idea but reports that he has not
  succeeded with it for a complete feature or prototype, and that it can turn into many hours of chatting
  with an LLM — though it has sometimes helped him abandon a project early once he saw how much it would
  take.
- Lee Boonstra's post on documenting prompts is described as the only post Mbanugo had seen on that
  topic; he says he already saves prompts to review which ones worked best, but without structure.
- Seth Godin's "ChatGPT is dumber than it looks" is summarized as a reminder that LLMs are not as smart as
  many think, with the advice to create patterns and processes in which the LLM is used as a tool.
- The post closes by quoting an excerpt from a later Seth Godin post: "When you get stuck, first ask
  Claude, then ask a human."
