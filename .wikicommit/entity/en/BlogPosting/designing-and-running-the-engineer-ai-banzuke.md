This post, by a manager in the AI Driven Promotion Office at [[Organization/cyberagent]], explains the
[[DefinedTerm/engineer-ai-banzuke]], a version of the company's AI Banzuke built specifically for development
organizations. The company-wide AI Banzuke ranks organizations' AI use on a sumo-style scale; the author argues
that viewpoints such as revenue, cost reduction and contribution to business KPIs cannot capture how mature a
development organization's AI use is, because an organization where individuals use generative AI in daily
coding and one that has built AI agents into its whole development process are both "using AI" while facing
very different next steps.

The post describes how the assessment was designed — two axes, fourteen items and seven ranks — how the first
round assessed 44 development organizations through self-reporting, evidence and deliberation, what the
operators observed, and what they intend to do before the second round.

## Key Points

- The engineer version was designed as a shared language for questions such as how far AI is built into the
  development process, whether AI is an assistive tool or acts autonomously, whether roles between humans and
  AI are clear, and whether AI goals connect to technical strategy, in support of CyberAgent's stated aim of
  automating the development processes across the company by 2028.
- It scores organizations on "engineering quality and technical maturity" and "promotion structure and
  technical culture", because the author holds that both technically advanced use and the ability to spread
  it reproducibly across an organization are needed.
- Results are expressed as seven ranks named after sumo ranks, from makushita (use limited to individuals) to
  yokozuna (an AI-first development organization), which the author says made positions easier to share
  intuitively than a plain score.
- Organizations reported their own status and supplied supporting evidence, which the operators checked
  against the evaluation items, with a deliberation council settling cases where needed; the author says the
  aim was for every organization to understand why it received its rank and what to improve next.
- The first round showed that many development organizations had already begun experimenting with AI well
  beyond implementation and review support, while how to turn individual or team practices into the
  organization's standard development process was a theme common to many of them.
- The author argues that AI adoption must move from individual productivity gains to redesigning the
  development process — revisiting how specifications, reviews, tests and knowledge sharing are done on the
  assumption that AI is present, rather than adding AI to the existing flow.
- For spreading practices, the operators intend to go beyond sharing case studies to explaining why a practice
  worked, what conditions make it reproducible and where to start small; the second round was planned for
  announcement in October 2026.

## Context

The post is the operators' own account of an internal programme at a single company, and its observations are
qualitative. It complements CyberAgent's separate description of the company-wide AI Banzuke, which it points
to for the overall background, and sits alongside the company's internal [[DefinedTerm/ai-maturity-levels]]
model as another way CyberAgent describes AI-adoption maturity; the post itself does not relate the two. The
author acknowledges that a ranking can generate enthusiasm but also risks the evaluation taking on a life of
its own, and frames the programme as aimed at helping each organization take its next step.
