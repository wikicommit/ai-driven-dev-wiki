A post published on Simon Willison's Weblog on 1 May 2025, arguing that two forthcoming books apply the term [[DefinedTerm/vibe-coding]] in their titles in a sense opposite to what it means. Willison restates the definition he takes to be correct — vibe coding is not "using AI tools to help write code" but "generating code with AI without caring about the code that is produced" — and calls the point "a hill I am willing to die on".

The post is a primary-source objection written by Willison himself, at a point where he says he considers the argument already lost. Beyond the complaint, it makes a positive argument: that vibe coding names something genuinely useful for non-developers, and that the widening of the term has cost the industry a name for it.

## Key Points

- Vibe coding means generating code with AI without caring about the code that is produced — not using AI tools as part of a process for responsibly building production code.
- Two forthcoming books use the term in their titles in the sense Willison rejects: [[Book/vibe-coding]] by Gene Kim and Steve Yegge, published by IT Revolution and subtitled "Building Production-Grade Software With GenAI, Chat, Agents, and Beyond" — which Willison calls "exactly what vibe coding is not" — and [[Book/vibe-coding-the-future-of-programming]] by Addy Osmani, published by O'Reilly Media, which he says likewise talks about how professional engineers can integrate AI-assisted coding tools into their workflow.
- The term was coined by [[Person/andrej-karpathy]] on 6 February 2025, 84 days before the post. Willison quotes Karpathy's tweet at length, emphasising two phrases: that you "forget that the code even exists", and that it is "not too bad for throwaway weekend projects".
- Willison also argues that the authors and publishers did not read to the end of the tweet before publishing books about the term.
- His positive argument is that vibe coding is for people who are not software developers and do not want to become developers. He describes this as a rich, deep topic — which projects can be built this way, how to avoid traps around security, privacy, reliability and the risk of over-spending, and how to navigate what is achievable versus impossible — and says a book aimed at that audience could be a genuine bestseller.
- He concedes he has lost the argument, attributing the shift to semantic diffusion, which he calls an unstoppable force.

## Context

The post continues Willison's earlier writing on the same subject, which he links to from its opening paragraph. His stated perspective is that the misuse is not a harmless drift but a loss: because the term was taken, a book for non-developers now needs a new "buzzy term" despite the perfect one already existing.

An update dated 4 September 2025, appended above the original text, notes that Osmani's book had since been retitled *Beyond Vibe Coding: From Coder to AI-Era Developer*, which Willison calls a much better title.
