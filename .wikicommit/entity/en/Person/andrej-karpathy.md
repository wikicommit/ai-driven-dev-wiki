Andrej Karpathy coined the term [[DefinedTerm/vibe-coding]] on 6 February 2025, in a tweet describing "a new kind of coding" in which, as he put it, you "fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget that the code even exists". He attributed its feasibility to LLMs getting "too good", naming Cursor Composer with Sonnet as an example, and said he talked to the tool via SuperWhisper rather than touching the keyboard much.

Karpathy qualified the approach in the same tweet, calling it "not too bad for throwaway weekend projects, but still quite amusing" — a qualification [[Person/simon-willison]] later argued the authors and publishers of two forthcoming books had missed by not reading to the end of the tweet.

## Background

The tweet describes Karpathy's own working practice at the time: asking for "the dumbest things" he was too lazy to do himself, always choosing "Accept All" and no longer reading the diffs, pasting error messages back in with no comment, and working around bugs the model could not fix by asking for random changes until they went away. He characterised the result as "not really coding — I just see stuff, say stuff, run stuff, and copy paste stuff, and it mostly works".

## Works & Achievements

That February 2025 tweet is the origin of the term vibe coding. Willison notes that within 84 days of the tweet, two forthcoming books were using the term in a sense contrary to Karpathy's description, which he attributes to three authors and the staff of two publishers not reading to the end of the tweet.
