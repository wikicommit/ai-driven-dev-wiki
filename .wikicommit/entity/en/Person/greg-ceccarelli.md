Greg Ceccarelli is a co-founder of [[Organization/specstory]] and the author of [[Report/beyond-code-centric]]. His argument there is the reason he appears on this wiki: that with agents able to generate code on demand, the constraint on software has moved from typing speed to clarity of thought, and that the methodologies the industry runs on — Waterfall, Agile, GitFlow — all break because each assumes a human will interpret an ambiguous instruction rather than act on it literally.

## Works & Achievements

His whitepaper of 15 June 2025 sets out the abstraction shift and the case for trunk-based development with versioned specifications, and describes [[DefinedTerm/specflow]] as the workflow SpecStory built on that basis.

Two positions in it are distinctly his rather than restatements of the surrounding literature. The first is a reading of Robert C. Martin: rather than treating "specifying requirements so precisely that a machine can execute them is programming" as an argument against the end of code, he takes it as newly literal, so that "the specification is still code, just a different kind". The second is his refusal to declare the problem solved by his own method — he writes that until better intent-centric tools reduce the micro-decisions the approach demands, "spec-driven development with agents remains powerful in theory but insufficient in practice".

He also states a role prediction: that the most valuable people become the clearest thinkers, conductors who orchestrate agents to build systems that once required much larger teams, and that senior developers must now teach specification and architecture rather than syntax.
