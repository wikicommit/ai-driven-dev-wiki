Gene Kim is a researcher and author, described in [[Report/state-of-ai-assisted-software-development-2025]] — for which he wrote the foreword — as "Researcher, Vibe Coder, Co-author of Vibe Coding, The Phoenix Project, DevOps Handbook, Accelerate". That self-description places him at the junction this wiki covers: a figure from the DevOps research lineage who now identifies with [[DefinedTerm/vibe-coding]] as a practice.

## Background

The episode he recounts himself is the origin of [[Organization/dora]]. In 2013 he had, as he puts it, "the privilege of working with Dr. Nicole Forsgren and Jez Humble on the State of DevOps research", work that became the basis for the DevOps Research and Assessment group.

His account of what that research showed is the argument he has carried forward: that doing multiple deployments per day was not a crazy idea, that reliability seemed to require smaller and more frequent deployments, and — the part he emphasises as most exciting — that "you didn't have to be in a startup or in Silicon Valley", needing only great technical practices such as automated builds, testing and deployments and proactive production telemetry, an architecture enabling independence of action, and a culture of learning. He notes how far the ground has moved since, observing that just over a decade ago software deployments "were dangerous and complex", requiring meticulous planning and approvals and often hundreds of risky, error-prone manual steps.

On the present research his stated position is enthusiasm rather than caution: "I'm so excited by how this year's research helps us better understand how we can use AI to improve software."
