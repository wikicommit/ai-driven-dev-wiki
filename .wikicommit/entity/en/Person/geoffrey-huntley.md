Geoffrey Huntley is an engineer and writer who named [[DefinedTerm/ralph]], the technique of running a coding agent in an unattended shell loop with a fixed prompt file. He set it out in [[BlogPosting/ralph-wiggum-as-a-software-engineer]] on 14 July 2025, publishing the prompts he was using at the time along with the failure modes and corrections behind them.

## Background

Huntley writes on his own site under an AI tag, and offers workshops and speaking. His writing on agentic coding is cumulative: the Ralph post treats several of his earlier pieces — on writing specifications through conversation with an agent, on delegating expensive work to subagents, on autoregressive failure modes, and on the gap between advertised and real context window sizes — as prerequisites rather than restating them.

He describes teaching Ralph to engineers in San Francisco, and relays that one of them applied it on their next contract with, in his account, an unusually high return.

## Works & Achievements

His principal demonstration of Ralph is CURSED, a programming language and compiler he says was built by the technique. He states that Ralph was able both to build the language and to program in it despite the language not existing in the model's training data, and describes his ultimate goal for the project as a self-hosting compiler with a full standard library written in the language itself.

Huntley's stated position on what this implies is deliberately two-sided. He argues Ralph can displace a large majority of software engineering work as currently practised for greenfield projects, and simultaneously that none of it is possible without senior expertise guiding the loop — writing that anyone claiming a tool can do 100% of the work without an engineer is "peddling horseshit."
