A Claw, in the generic sense Simon Willison uses in [[BlogPosting/2026-in-llms-so-far]], is a piece
of personal AI agent software of the kind [[SoftwareApplication/openclaw]] introduced: a weird little
agent that does useful things on its owner's behalf. Willison says OpenClaw effectively defined a new
category of software and that "Claw" is the generic term for it, giving NanoClaw, IronClaw and PicoClaw
as other examples. Underneath, on his account, a Claw is really just a coding agent wearing a less
threatening hat: it works much the same way as one, writing and then executing code on the user's
computer to get things done.

## Usage

Willison notes that such software is now being rebranded as "personal agents" or "general agents",
while he still prefers to think of them as Claws. He reports that Apple stores in the Bay Area sold
out of Mac minis because so many people were buying them to run OpenClaw, and relays Drew Breunig's
explanation: that a Claw is a digital pet, and the Mac mini is the aquarium you keep it in. Of the
OpenClaw install parties held in China in March 2026, at which people who were not technical queued
for help getting Claws onto their own devices, he says they proved real market demand for this class
of personal agent.

Because a Claw has the same capabilities as a [[DefinedTerm/ai-coding-agent]] but is handed to people
who are not developers, Willison describes a race to build the first **safe Claw** — one regular
people can be given without instantly shooting themselves in the foot. He names Meta's Muse, released
weeks before his talk, as a contender that is taking off with consumers, and says he is not yet
convinced it cannot still be misused that way.
