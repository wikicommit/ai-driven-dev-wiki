The paradox of supervision is the name [[Organization/anthropic]]'s study
[[Report/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic]] gives to one reason the atrophy of coding skills is
concerning: effectively using Claude requires supervising it, and supervising Claude requires the very
coding skills that may atrophy from AI overuse. It is the report's link between
[[DefinedTerm/skill-atrophy]] — skills getting less practice as more work is delegated — and the active
supervision and validation that its respondents describe as part of using Claude.

## Usage

The report uses the term in its account of interviews with Anthropic engineers and researchers about
skills getting less practice, and ties it back to its survey finding that more than half of respondents
could "fully delegate" only 0–20% of their work to Claude, with engineers describing active validation of
its output, particularly for complex or high-stakes work. One interviewee put the concern this way: they
worried much more about "the oversight and supervision problem" than about their skill set as such,
because skills that atrophy or fail to develop would be a problem mainly for their ability to use AI
safely on the tasks they cared about, rather than for their ability to do those tasks independently.

The study also records how some engineers respond: deliberately practising without AI from time to time,
even when Claude could solve the problem, to keep themselves sharp. A senior engineer said they would be
more worried if they were more junior, since they had built the ability to judge what an answer should
look like by doing software engineering "the hard way," and growing that ability while relying on the
model would take deliberate effort. The report presents these as the views of its interviewees, and notes
that engineers were divided on how much the underlying skill loss matters.
