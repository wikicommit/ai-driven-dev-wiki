Tokenmaxxing is the name used in [[BlogPosting/2026-in-llms-so-far]] for a trend that began in February
2026: large employers pushing for maximal use of AI by their staff. Simon Willison illustrates it with
headlines about Meta making AI adoption a formal part of performance reviews, Microsoft wanting almost
every employee to use AI, and Uber boasting that 90% of its engineers were using AI in their workflows.
A few months later the same companies were reported cracking down on employee token use, telling
engineers that tokenmaxxing was "not what we are optimizing for", and capping AI spending.

## Usage

Willison's explanation for the reversal is cost: tokenmaxxing went straight up and straight back down
because agents turned out to be expensive. Where a year earlier it was hard to spend more than $50 on
AI tokens for lack of anything interesting to do with them, he says that once agents took off it became
possible to spend $1,000 in a day doing real work. He presents the episode as part of the evidence that
AI hit product-market fit in 2026, primarily through coding agents.

The term describes an organizational pattern rather than an individual one; Willison's name for the
individual counterpart — the compulsion to keep one's own agents busy — is [[DefinedTerm/ai-mania]].
