**in-IDE HAX** — Human-AI Experience in Integrated Development Environment — names the field that
studies the evolving dynamics of Human-Computer Interaction in AI-assisted coding environments. The
term marks a shift of focus rather than a new tool: where Human-Computer Interaction asks how users
interact with software and tools, Human-AI Experience is proposed for the case where AI is integrated
into development workflows and acts as an active collaborator rather than just a tool, changing how
developers work and interact with their environment.

## Usage

The term is used by [[ScholarlyArticle/human-ai-experience-in-integrated-development-environments]],
which argues that research in the area remains fragmented and sets out to give it a unified overview.
That review makes the scope explicit by defining what counts as an IDE for the purpose: any workspace
that simultaneously offers code editing, immediate execution or preview, and contextual AI feedback
in the same window. Traditional desktop IDEs and notebook-style environments both satisfy all three
criteria, as do plug-ins that embed a full editor and run code inline; tools that push AI suggestions
outside the primary editing surface — the review's examples are web explainers and offline static
analysers — are excluded. The boundary is what separates in-IDE HAX from the broader question of how
AI helps in software engineering: the focus is on what happens in the very place where code is
written, inspected or tested.

The same review organises the field's findings into three dimensions, derived through a staged
qualitative coding procedure and carried over from an earlier survey by the same group:

- **Impact** — the effects of AI assistance on developers, tasks and workflows.
- **Design** — how AI is integrated into the IDE and interacted with.
- **Quality** — properties of AI outputs such as correctness, security and readability.

The review reports that its expanded corpus confirmed the adequacy of this three-part framing and
added finer subscopes within each dimension without introducing any new top-level dimension. It also
notes the practical consequence of studying experience rather than capability: a subset of the
literature conceptualises programming with AI as a form of pair programming, treating the system as a
collaborative partner, which the review reads as a move from isolated completions toward co-creative
workflows and as raising concrete needs for training in model interaction and oversight in
professional teams, and for scaffolds that keep learners in control in educational settings.
